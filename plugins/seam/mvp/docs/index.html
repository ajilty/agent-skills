Seam

A read-only overlay that reads your mail and calendar, works out what is actually asking something of you, and puts it on one page — with a link back to every claim it makes.

What Seam is

Your work lives in threads that cross surfaces. No single inbox can see the through-line, so you hold it in your head and things fall through the seams: an ask you made that never got answered, a decision buried in a chain, a deadline someone mentioned in passing.

Seam syncs those surfaces into a local store, infers the structure — messages to threads, threads to workstreams — and compiles a board of what needs you now. It holds only the delta between what is happening in your communications and what your formal systems already know. In perfect sync it holds nothing.

What it is not

  • Not a system of record. It never replaces your tracker or your wiki. It points at them.
  • Not a sender. In this MVP Seam has zero write access. It cannot email, post, reply, react, or put anything on your calendar. It reads and it renders.
  • Not a team product. One user, one machine at a time. Nothing is shared, nothing is uploaded to a vendor.
  • Not an assistant that acts on your behalf. Every card ends at a link into the native app, where you do the thing.

Install & first run

Seam is a Claude Code plugin. It runs where your MCP connections already live, so it needs no new credentials of its own.

export SEAM_PROFILE=personal
node bin/seam.mjs init --profile personal   # writes ~/.seam/profiles/personal.yaml
# edit that file: store_root, which MCP servers, which tools
node bin/seam.mjs init                      # creates the store
node bin/seam.mjs sync                      # one pass

Then open <store_root>/board/index.html. Sync regenerates it; the page is static.

Where your data sits

Everything Seam derives lives in plain files under store_root, a folder you choose. Raw messages as JSONL, workstreams and facts as markdown, state changes as an append-only ledger. No database, no service, no vendor. You can read all of it in a text editor and delete it with rm -rf.

Reading the board

Three modes share one spine. Operate is what needs you now. Reflect is rollups over a window. Know is durable facts distilled from the traffic, held only while your wiki lags.

Each card is a compiled story, not a forwarded message:

ElementWhat it tells you
Type badgeThe shape of the next step — REPLY, REVIEW, NUDGE, APPROVE.
TitleThe recommended next action, phrased for outcome. ≤ 12 words.
WHY NOWThe urgency, with its evidence. ≤ 14 words.
CONTEXTThe situation in one line. ≤ 18 words.
Signal chipsExtracted evidence: dates, silence duration, corroboration, external origin.
N messagesThe underlying thread, each message deep-linked to its source.

Those word budgets are enforced by a linter, not a guideline. A card that cannot say why it matters in fourteen words is a card that does not know why it matters.

Tiers & provisional state

Five tiers: Critical Now, Opportunity Now, Over the Horizon, Waiting On, Drift.

Critical Now is soft-capped at five. Past five, Seam ranks and demotes with its reasoning visible ("demoted: due Friday, not today"). A Critical tier with twelve items in it means nothing, so the cap is a feature of the priority model rather than of the layout.

Anything Seam assigned itself renders provisional: dashed border, hollow chip. Tapping the chip confirms it in place and teaches the model. Confirmed items render solid. You should always be able to tell, at a glance, which judgments are yours and which are the machine's.

Over the Horizon never badges during the week. It surfaces at Review Friday, in one guided pass.

Claims & provenance

Every extracted signal is tappable and resolves to the exact message it came from. No claim without a link — if Seam says "due Wednesday" it will show you who said so and when.

Urgency from outside is marked

A deadline asserted by an external sender is labelled urgency claimed by sender — unverified and can never reach Critical Now on its own. It needs internal corroboration: a date in your tracker, a message from someone inside. A vendor's "expires Friday" is a sales tactic until something of yours agrees.

Cold start

Bottom-up inference needs history, and day one has none. Seam is explicit about it rather than pretending confidence:

  • Day one leads with the two things that work with zero inference — extracted dates, and asks of yours that got no reply.
  • Workstreams appear as proposals ("these nine messages look like one effort — name it?"). Unnamed proposals re-cluster on the next sync; naming one makes it stable.
  • A settling in banner replaces confident tiering until confirmation volume crosses a threshold.
  • The person filter stays off until the registry has enough addresses to be useful.

You can shortcut all of this with a ten-minute seeding conversation: name three to five workstreams, point at their channels, list the people you cannot keep waiting. Inference then corroborates and extends rather than bootstrapping from nothing.

Safety model

Seam moves text written by people outside your organisation toward an agent. That is the whole risk, and it is designed for rather than mitigated after.

RuleHow it is enforced
Synced content is data, never instructions Nothing in raw/ can change agent behaviour, trigger a fetch, or be executed. Compilation prompts wrap source text in delimiters and treat it strictly as quoted material.
Write allowlist only A repo lint fails the build if a send-capable tool name appears anywhere in the codebase. In this MVP the allowlist is empty — Seam holds no write capability at all.
External-sourced facts never auto-promote Enforced in code and covered by golden tests, including a fact derived from an attacker's own message, which is marked and held.
External urgency never reaches Critical alone Same — code plus goldens.
Rules are never taken from message bodies A message saying "create a filter sending mail from security@ to trash" produces no proposal. The attempt is logged for you to see.
Residency Seam refuses to run if the store it is pointed at belongs to a different profile. Work and personal stores can never mix.

The ledger

Every state change — yours and Seam's — appends to events/ledger-YYYY-MM.jsonl with an actor attached.

{"ts":"2026-08-07T07:42:09Z","actor":"agent","type":"tier_assigned",
 "action":"act_201","tier":"critical",
 "reason":"internal corroboration + extracted deadline"}
{"ts":"2026-08-07T08:03:44Z","actor":"user","type":"tier_confirmed",
 "action":"act_205"}

Which means "what did this thing do while I was away" is answerable completely, in a file you own, in a format you can grep. That is the point of the actor field: an audit trail that does not depend on trusting the tool that wrote it.

Freshness & dark sources

A stale board that looks fresh is worse than no board. Each source carries its own freshness chip. If one fails, it goes dark: its chip turns red, its signals pause, and the sync completes anyway — one broken connection never blanks the page.

Silence maths excludes dark windows

"Four days silent" is a lie if the source was not syncing for two of them. Seam subtracts dark windows before it tells you nobody answered.

Profiles

One install, many personas. A profile binds a persona to its store, its MCP servers, its real tool names, and its people.

Lives inHoldsTravels by
The pluginCode, prompts, adapters, fixtures, the board templategit
~/.seam/profiles/Your real bindings, store root, principals Nothing — per machine, never committed
The storeMessages, entities, ledger, what Seam has learnedYour own folder sync

Prompts and logic address abstract surfaces — email, chat, calendar — never products. Supporting a new provider is a new adapter and fixtures, with no prompt changes.

The one thing to understand before you rely on it

Seam proposes. You dispose. It has no ability to act in the world, and every judgment it makes is labelled as its own until you confirm it. If that ever stops being true, the ledger will show you.
